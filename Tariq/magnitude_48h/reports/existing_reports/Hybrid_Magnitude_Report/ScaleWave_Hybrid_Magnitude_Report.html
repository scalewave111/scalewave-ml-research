How large could the next 48-hour move be?

A trader-friendly research summary

Our research forecasts the size of the change from the current price to the price 48 hours later, regardless of whether that change is up or down. It is intended to add information about potential move size.

Average forecast error: 180.798518
Metric: MAE, in log basis points. Status: development candidate. Fresh-data confirmation is still pending.

Read the number correctly

180.799 is the average miss across historical predictions. It is not today's forecast, a percentage accuracy or a confidence level. Each timestamp receives its own move-size estimate.

We express moves in log basis points, calculated as 10,000 times the absolute log of the future-price/current-price ratio. For small moves, 100 log basis points is roughly a 1% price change.

The forecast concerns the price at the end of 48 hours. It does not describe the highest and lowest prices along the way. By itself it cannot provide an intraperiod trading range, a stop distance, a direction call or an extreme-move probability.

Seven inputs, explained as market activity

The market information behind the forecast

Market measure and windowWhat it tells us
Liquidation activity: 15mHow often forced position closures appear in the supplied cross-asset activity measure.
Liquidation activity: 5mA shorter view of the same activity, helping describe recent changes.
Trading activity: 1hHow busy trading is compared with its own history over the longer of our three activity windows.
Trading activity: 15mA more recent view of trading activity.
Trading activity: 5mThe shortest activity window; this was the last addition that improved average error.
Liquidation spread across venues: 1hWhether liquidation activity is spread across platforms or concentrated in fewer places.
Liquidation clustering through time: 1hWhether liquidations arrive in bursts or are more evenly spread through the window.

The two liquidation measures describe how often activity appears, not the amount liquidated. The activity inputs do not, by themselves, identify buy versus sell pressure. These descriptions explain the intended meaning of the supplied feature names.

How the two forecasts work together

Starting estimate: two liquidation measures produce a typical move-size forecast.
Pattern estimate: Random Forests use all seven inputs to look for relationships between market conditions and later move size.
Combined estimate: we blend the two forecasts. Earlier historical results decide how much weight to give the pattern estimate.

The pattern estimate averages three forests, each containing 128 decision trees. In plain language, it combines many small rules learned from historical examples. Averaging three versions reduces dependence on one random training sample.

Why these inputs earned a place

We first improved the liquidation forecast by updating it over time, then combining it with the forest forecast. Tuning the forests and adding trading activity, venue fragmentation and time clustering produced further gains.

The latest accepted addition was 5-minute trading activity. It reduced average error from 180.815207 to 180.798518. It helped all three periods where the forest contributes; the fourth was unchanged.

Each feature window describes the input information. A 5-minute input still contributes to our 48-hour forecast. It does not turn the model into a 5-minute forecast.

A feature's inclusion is evidence that it helped during development. It does not establish a universal trading rule such as "high liquidation activity always causes a large move." The model's relationships depend on the other inputs and the period.

How much trust should we place in it?

Reading the research like a trader

Common ML terms, translated

Target / magnitudeThe size of the eventual 48-hour endpoint move we are trying to forecast.

Spearman ICHow well forecasts rank larger versus smaller moves. It is not trade win rate or confidence.
PercentileWhere a market reading ranks against its past. A 90th-percentile reading is not a 90% chance of a price move.
Walk-forward refittingUpdate the forecasting rules using past data, then assess the following period.
Validation / fresh confirmationEarlier data used to choose settings / separate data used later to check whether results hold up.

Our current Spearman IC is 0.078527: positive but small. MAE remains the deciding score; a better ranking score alone does not qualify a new model for promotion.

What is ready, and what comes next?

From research result to trading use

The three stages in the screenshots

StageMeaning and our current position
1. Research evidenceOur current work: compare forecast errors, useful inputs and performance across time. Status: development candidate.
2. Standard forecast outputAt each timestamp, supply a structured forecast. Our model can supply the move-size component; direction and extreme-move probabilities need separate evaluated components.
3. Trading decisionsDecide whether and how to trade using the forecasts, costs and risk limits. This requires its own testing; it has not been established by our MAE results.

What a future forecast record should contain

Timestamp and horizon: when the forecast was made and its 48-hour endpoint.
Move-size estimate: the model's estimate in log basis points.
Market context: relevant activity readings and any conditions supported by research.
Evidence status: development candidate, awaiting fresh-data confirmation.

Direction probabilities and the chance of an extreme move should be added only after those components are developed and tested. A single move-size estimate does not establish those probabilities.

What the completed feature search tells us

All eligible features in the current PDF inventory have now been tested individually along our development path. The final eight additions did not beat MAE 180.798518. Six other features were set aside because they lack sufficient usable past readings.

This exhausts that individual screening list. It does not establish that all useful market information has been captured. Some features were tested against earlier versions of the model; combinations, replacements and inputs outside the PDF remain possibilities.

The next research step

Compare alternative models using the same seven inputs and evaluation dates. A small neural network or another tree model must earn its place through lower verified error. Combination tests remain deferred. Any 24-hour, 4-hour or 15-minute version needs its own target, fitted models and benchmark.

Key takeaway
We have improved a historical 48-hour move-size forecast. Its useful role is potential market context. The next requirements are stronger evidence about when it works, confirmation on fresh data, and separate testing of any trading rules built around it.
